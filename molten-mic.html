Molten Mic

Four heat treatments for the 54pt voice button. Each one keeps the original dark glass disk and mic glyph, then adds its own fire. Hover a button to warm it, click it to start a simulated voice, and the effect follows the level.

Where they sit

Each design placed by how much it holds back versus how hard it performs, and by how hot it reads. Click a point to jump to its card.

What I'd ship

  1. 02 Liquid Glass at rest. It reads as the same Figma disk from across the room, and the heat only shows up close. It sits next to system UI without shouting.
  2. 06 Torch while listening. Cross-fade into the burning glyph when the mic opens, so the flames are the "I hear you" cue and rise with your voice. Trim the particle count for toolbar use.
  3. 05 Basalt as an alternate skin. The same heat in a calmer, textural form, good for a themed or dark-mode variant.
  4. 01 Obsidian Core for reduced motion. A static magma glow under the glass, no particles.

Behind the button

Five ways to put the heat into the screen itself. Each background is a live liquid gradient in burn colours, and each one puts the button somewhere different relative to the light. Every mic here is live too.

Every effect runs in one 2D canvas per button plus CSS: the glass edge shine is a conic gradient spun with @property, the heat haze on 06 is an SVG displacement filter, and the cracks on 05 render at 96px and upscale. The screen backgrounds are one small WebGL shader with five modes, plus film grain. Voice level is simulated. The mic glyph is redrawn to SF Symbols mic.fill proportions because the Figma "Search Tab" layer exports empty.
